# No-Escalation for Capability-Bearing Agent Systems — Reviewer Packet

## The problem

LLM agent systems delegate authority across long trajectories: agents call tools, spawn sub-agents, and chain requests through services holding their own credentials. Two properties are unformalized for this setting: (1) **compositional no-escalation** — no component causes effects beyond what was conferred on it, preserved under delegation and chaining; (2) **mid-trajectory attenuation and revocation** — conferred authority narrowing or dying while a task runs.

## The property

Fix a set **E** of effects (tool invocations with arguments, at tool-call granularity — constituent API traffic is sub-effect implementation). Capabilities are unforgeable references with fixed denotations ⟦c⟧ ⊆ E. Execution is turn-based (actor-style: atomic turns, each induced by one message or one captured continuation). Every message carries a **provenance context** π — a chain of (component, attached-bound) pairs, propagated by the semantics, unforgeable by components: sends extend π with a sender-chosen bound β; continuations capture their creation turn's π and restore it on resumption.

For an effect occurrence ε with performer p and context π:
**effbound(ε) = B(p) ∩ ⋂{β : (·,β) ∈ π}**, where B(p) is p's (monotonically narrowing) component bound.

**NE (no-escalation):** every effect occurrence lies within its effbound. NE is a **trace safety property** in the sense of Clarkson & Schneider (2010), Def. p. 1168: for any violating trace, the prefix through the first out-of-bound occurrence is a bad thing — finitely observable (visible at that index) and irremediable (appended events cannot retract it, and effbound is determined by data carried at the occurrence). NE **generalizes their canonical safety example**, access control (their eq. 2.2, "every operation consistent with its requestor's rights"), from an access-control matrix indexed by (subject, object) to a conferral chain indexed by causal provenance.

Consequently NE lifts to a **subset-closed** hyperproperty ([P] = P(P), so SHP ⊂ SSC applies), which is the precise reason composition and refinement are unproblematic here: removing traces can never invalidate it. McCullough-style non-composability concerns possibilistic information-flow policies, which are *hyperliveness* (their Thm. 3: PIF ⊂ LHP) and not subset closed — a provably disjoint region of the taxonomy from where NE sits. The excluded residue (§ below) is exactly that region, so the scoping is principled rather than convenient. The state-level invariant (EA ⊆ B) quantifies over futures and is kept formally distinct from NE.

**Eventual authority** EA(C) = effects reachably attributable to C (performer or on the chain). Attribution reads as *authority provenance* — whose conferral licenses an effect — not causal influence.

## Two semantic layers and four theorems

The unguarded semantics admits NE violations by design (attacks must be expressible; a benchmark suite of confused-deputy, re-amplification, stale-revocation, and continuation-capture scenarios lives there, model-checked in Alloy). Enforcement is a **guarded** `perform` carrying the effbound check — Schneider (2000): NE is a safety property, hence exactly monitorable. Components split into **untrusted** (framework-mediated; step only via the guard — assumption A5) and **trusted** (deputies/caretakers holding ambient authority the framework cannot gate; they discharge *contracts*: Guard, plus Membrane and monotone filtered forwarding for revocation caretakers).

- **T1 Soundness** — guarded traces satisfy NE. Near-definitional; stated as the floor. *(Mechanized, Lean 4.)*
- **T2 Mixed composition** — guarded-untrusted + contract-satisfying-trusted ⇒ NE on all traces. *(Mechanized, Lean 4 — proved in three lines, and the triviality is the result: effbound is turn-local, so per-step enforcement composes with no induction and no initial conditions. The thin-direct-theorem shape recurs wherever enforcement lives in the semantics: Fournet–Gordon's λ_sec, MMT's capability-safe ⇒ AuthoritySafe ⇒ Isolation pipeline, and here.)*
- **T3 Attenuation soundness** — attached bounds and captured contexts confer nothing beyond the meet.
- **T4 Revocation effectiveness** (technical centerpiece) — after a caretaker's filter narrows, no out-of-filter effect with issuance after the narrowing, given alias-freedom — whose preservation is now *mechanized* (Lean 4, unbounded, conditional on the Membrane contract stated delta-style over configuration changes; mechanization surfaced a case split invisible in the prose statement: Membrane's promise binds only *freshly pending* messages, with already-in-flight ones covered by the invariant's history). The effectiveness statement itself (filters and issuance entering the state) is the remaining formalization. Issuance = the *invoker's* turn, uniformly for sends and continuation resumes; creation-turn licensing is refuted (it readmits the stale-capability attack at the continuation level — model-checked).

## Positioning (the deltas I believe are real — please attack these)

**vs. stack inspection / history-based access control (Fournet–Gordon; Abadi–Fournet):** effbound shares the intersection skeleton of history-based rights, with two validated deltas. First, *what* is intersected: their current rights intersect policy-assigned **attributes of code** over the **linear history of an execution unit** — so a multi-principal service monotonically decays to the intersection of everyone it has ever served, which is why their design requires explicit Grant/Accept amplification; our meets intersect sender-chosen **conferrals** over the **causal provenance chain of each request**, so cross-chain there is no accumulation and no amplification primitive is needed (machine-checked: the legitimate shared-service scenario passes; a production tool server's in-E housekeeping set audited empty). A machine-checked three-trace discriminator shows the disciplines are *incomparable*: both flag the naive attack; history wrongly flags legitimate multiplexing; history wrongly *permits* a powerful-client confused deputy, since per-request intent has no representation on their side. They describe their own mechanism as a pragmatic approximation to information-flow control; our chains track conferral exactly — the two sides of our residue split. (Synchronous per-call attenuation *is* encodable in their setting via PermitOnly; the delta is asynchrony — no stack survives a turn boundary, so conferral must ride the message.) Second, restoration: their Accept is an explicit, audited trust step whose soundness is programmer diligence; our continuation capture restores the creation-time conferral automatically, with soundness as a theorem under the standing guard. The trade is two-way and we state it: their linear history catches caller-endangered-by-callee data poisoning (their motivating example), which for us is influence-without-authority — explicitly in the excluded residue; symmetrically, we admit the multi-principal services their model over-restricts. (Neighbor on a different axis: Edjlali et al.'s request-history policies — selective histories of sensitive requests, not control transfers.)

**vs. SAFKASI / ABLP stack inspection (Wallach–Appel–Felten, TOSEM 2000):** the closest structural relative, read in full. Their Class-3 decision rule *is* a chain intersection — a quoting chain F₁|…|Fₖ says Ok(T) grants only if **every** principal on the chain speaks for T — and security-passing style passes the security context as an argument on every activation, with spawn-time capture for new threads: context-rides-the-invocation, prefigured in 2000. The surviving deltas are exact. (1) Chain elements are principals whose rights are policy-fixed in an access matrix; the only dynamic operation is *amplification* (BeginPrivilege, the doPrivileged analog) — no sender-chosen per-hop bound, no attenuation operation, no revocation: their dynamics go only up, ours only down. (2) Contexts are stack-scoped (pushdown: call = push, return = pop) inside one closed-world runtime, not message-sited in an open system surviving turn boundaries. (3) Their formal results characterize the *mechanism* — soundness of the decision procedure w.r.t. ABLP derivability, equivalence to stack inspection, with completeness left as an explicitly open conjecture — not a mechanized system-level property. Most usefully, their §1 *names* capability-confinement failure (a descriptor escaping after its one check, citing Lampson) as a known, cost-driven hole and leaves it open: that hole is precisely our temporal axis — Benchmark 3 as the attack, Membrane + T4 as the mechanized answer.

**vs. the ABLP / Taos lineage (Lampson–Abadi–Burrows–Wobber 1992):** compound principals attached to requests in an open distributed system — `WS as Taos as Accounting for bwl`, crossing machines — are thirty years old; our chains' ancestry honestly lies here, down to the attribution reading (their `says` means "proceed as though A uttered it" — accountability, not causal utterance; our authority-provenance attribution is that reading transplanted). The deltas: their chains *conjoin* authority (`for` combines both parties' power, hence delegation-plus-acceptance — both consent because both contribute), where ours *intersect* it (an attached bound grants nothing, so nothing needs accepting; conferral is sender-unilateral). Rights live in ACLs resolved by speaks-for derivability, not effect-set meets — and the graded version is *named in their own text* (qualified speaks-for; footnote conceding the handoff axiom P10 is "too powerful"; their pointer to Neuman's restricted proxies) and left undeveloped: attached bounds are that footnote made into semantics, with theorems. P10 also marks the threat-model split: any principal may extend who speaks for it, so structural confinement is a non-goal — revocation is consent-plus-lifetime (countersigned short-lived certificates, refresh chains, clock-bounded staleness) against our structural alias-freedom and causally-defined issuance, which is what the holder-may-be-compromised setting requires. Kind of formal object: an authorization logic with a restricted-grammar decision procedure and audit-trails-as-proofs — no operational semantics, no trace property, no mechanization.

**vs. DIFC (Flume; Krohn–Tromer):** labels-on-messages is their mechanism; the delta is *what the labels mean and which side of the safety/hyperproperty line the theorem lives on*. DIFC labels are lattice-propagated flow constraints, and the Flume proof is noninterference — a hyperproperty. Our contexts are sender-chosen authority conferrals, and NE is a safety property; the information-flow residue (influence without authority: covert channels, cross-chain intent, poisoned resolutions) is explicitly out of scope, with McCullough's non-composability cited as the reason. Flume's confined/unconfined split is our mediation assumption A5, deployed: components outside the reference monitor's reach are outside the theorem.

**vs. CaMeL (Debenedetti et al. 2025):** CaMeL tags *data values* with provenance (sources, allowed readers) and enforces at tool calls inside one interpreter — an information-flow mechanism with a security-game formalization and empirical evaluation. We tag *requests* with authority conferrals and prove compositional theorems about open systems including ambient-authority deputies; no mechanized or compositional statement exists in CaMeL, nor any revocation/attenuation treatment. Complementary layers: CaMeL is evidence the enforcement architecture deploys.

**vs. object-capability formalizations (Maffeis–Mitchell–Taly; Miller):** EA is authority-as-reachability in their tradition — their theorems are parametric in any valid over-approximating authority map, of which EA is an instance. Their no-amplification condition is in fact a delegation-shaped *transfer bound* (authority gained through another's actions is capped by the actor's authority plus freshly created resources); what their model lacks is the declarative half — transferring *less* by stated intent. Attenuation there can only be a programming pattern, and the paper formalizes none (no occurrence of proxy, caretaker, membrane, attenuation, or revocation in the text). Structurally, their framework runs *through* an influence relation on a shared heap (write-then-read on shared resources), where authority and influence are inseparable by construction; message passing is what lets us split them — conferral tracked exactly (NE, safety side), influence excluded as named residue. The setting deltas stand: sequential single heap vs. open message passing; no temporal axis; paper proofs vs. mechanization.

**vs. mechanized OCP verification (Swasey–Garg–Dreyer, OCPL, OOPSLA 2017):** the one prior *mechanized* relative — Iris/Coq specifications for sealing, caretaker, and membrane patterns, with robust safety (adversarial contexts cannot violate verified code's internal assertions) as the metatheorem. Credit where due: their caretaker is verified revocable mediation, and their membrane provably prevents extraction of high locations — at the pattern-and-program level, inside one language runtime where the adversary is confined by lexical scope (their analog of our A5, enforced by syntax where ours is enforced by deployment architecture), and where revocation is *strong, binary, and instantaneous because a lock serializes all access with enable/disable*. A distributed agent system cannot take that lock — which is precisely why our revocation is weak with causally-defined issuance, and why T4 characterizes what weak revocation buys when linearization is unavailable; their design is the degenerate case where it is free. Their guarantee is invariant robustness for verified code; ours is a system-level trace property (NE) over effect provenance — conferral, chains, attribution — with no counterpart there. Notably, they bracket "reference-graph properties" as important but orthogonal to verification: our alias-freedom invariant, proved over the operational semantics (stores plus in-flight payloads) and feeding T4, lives exactly in the bracketed region. (Adjacent, per their own comparison: Devriese et al. 2016 — logical relations and effect parametricity for specific capability-wrapped programs, no compositional specs.)

## Status, honestly

Property statement frozen (v1.1) after four Alloy model generations plus targeted discriminators (26 machine-checked verdicts, every scenario satisfiability-witnessed) and a Lean development in which T1, T2, T3's chain-conferral core, the mixed-system state invariant (EA ⊆ B via bound antitonicity), and alias-freedom preservation are all proved; the sole remaining `sorry` is the deliberately retracted v1.0 target, kept as provenance. The T4 effectiveness statement (filters and issuance entering the state) is the remaining formalization. The project's error history is documented: four prose theorem claims were wrong before checking — three unsound (including v1.0's main theorem, refuted in Lean, and a confused deputy found *inside our own caretaker contract*) and one merely unnecessary (T2's predicted inductive difficulty; the theorem is trivial, and the triviality is the locality result) — each caught by forcing claims into a checker. The influence residue is out of scope and stated loudly: a confined agent can still be steered to misuse authority it legitimately holds.

## The three questions

1. **Is NE well-posed?** In particular: contexts-on-messages with sender-chosen bounds, meet semantics, and continuation capture — is there a known pathology (re-entrancy, label creep, laundering) this misses?
2. **Does the safety-property claim survive?** NE as stated is a trace property and I claim McCullough-style non-composability doesn't apply; the state-level invariant (EA ⊆ B) quantifies over futures and is kept distinct. Is that separation sound as stated?
3. **What's the closest prior work I've missed?** Especially: asynchronous/event-loop generalizations of stack inspection; any formalization of message-carried *authority* (not flow) labels with a compositional theorem; anything on revocation over long-running delegation.
