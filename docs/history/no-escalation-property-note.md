# No-Escalation for Capability-Bearing Agent Systems: Property Statement

**Status:** working note, v0.1 — definitions are candidates, not commitments.
**Purpose:** fix the formal property this project proves, before choosing a calculus or a proof assistant.

## 1. Scope and threat model

We consider systems of *components* (agents, sub-agents, tools) that hold capabilities, invoke tools, exchange messages, and spawn sub-components over long trajectories. The adversary controls the behavior of some components (including their strategy across many steps) but cannot forge capabilities. The property concerns **effect escalation only**: a component causing effects beyond what it was granted. Information-flow escalation (covert channels, signalling through permitted effects) is explicitly out of scope; see §6.

## 2. Effects and traces

Fix a set **E** of atomic observable effects. Candidate granularity: an effect is a tool invocation *together with its arguments* (plus spawns and sends). Coarser granularity (tool name only) makes attenuation inexpressible: "may read only /tmp/*" requires arguments to be part of the effect.

- A **trace** is a finite or infinite sequence over E, generated by a transition system TS = (S, s₀, →) whose transitions are labeled by (component, effect) pairs or by internal steps.
- **Open question (Q1):** is E finite, or structured (e.g., effects as pairs (op, arg) with arg drawn from an infinite domain)? Finite E suffices for the Alloy phase; the Lean development should permit structured E from the start.

## 3. Capabilities and authority

- A **capability** c is an unforgeable reference with denotation ⟦c⟧ ⊆ E, the effects it directly enables. Attenuation of c is a capability c′ with ⟦c′⟧ ⊆ ⟦c⟧.
- Each component C has a store σ(C) of held capabilities (part of the state).
- **Direct authority:** DA(C) = ⋃ { ⟦c⟧ : c ∈ σ(C) }. This is Miller's *permission* (Robust Composition; descending from Bishop & Snyder 1979, de jure transfer).
- **Eventual authority:** EA(C) = the set of effects e such that, from the current state, there exists a continuation of the system in which e occurs *and is attributable to C* (performed by C, or induced by a message chain originating from C). This is authority-as-reachability (cf. Maffeis, Mitchell & Taly 2010) and corresponds to de facto transfer.

DA is a function of the store; EA is a function of the whole transition system. The gap DA(C) ⊊ EA(C) is where the confused deputy lives, and the two are kept as distinct formal objects throughout.

**Open question (Q2 — attribution).** "Attributable to C" is doing hidden work. When deputy D performs effect e while servicing a request from C, the candidate resolutions are:

1. **Component-bound only:** e is checked against B(D) alone. Simple; makes the confused deputy *invisible* (the attack satisfies the property). Rejected unless the calculus separately forbids deputization.
2. **Provenance labels:** every message carries the originating component (or the meet of bounds along the request chain); an effect is checked against the bound carried by the request that induced it. Expressible; adds label machinery to the calculus; this is the CaMeL-style move.
3. **Capability-confinement:** no bounds on requests at all; instead the calculus is restricted so a deputy can only act on behalf of C using capabilities C passed in the request (no ambient authority in deputies). The classic ocap answer; strongest structural assumption.

Resolution of Q2 **is** the calculus design decision. Current lean: (3) as the core theorem's assumption, with (2) as the relaxation studied after the first proof works.

## 4. The property, at two levels

Each component C carries a **bound** B(C) ⊆ E as part of the state, with the *monotonicity rule*: every transition satisfies B′(C) ⊆ B(C) (bounds only narrow).

- **NE-T (trace-level no-escalation).** On every trace, every effect e attributed to component C at step i satisfies e ∈ Bᵢ(C).
  NE-T is prefix-closed and finitely refutable: a violation is a finite trace ending in one out-of-bound effect. It is a safety property in the Alpern–Schneider sense, a property of *individual traces* — not a hyperproperty — so McCullough-style non-composability of noninterference does not apply to it. (This claim must survive a re-read of McCullough 1987/88 and Clarkson–Schneider 2010 before the note is frozen.)
- **NE-S (state-level invariant).** In every reachable state, EA(C) ⊆ B(C) for every C.
  NE-S is a predicate on states involving quantification over futures; it is *not* a trace property. It is the inductive invariant: strictly stronger than NE-T.

**Lemma to prove first:** NE-S at all reachable states ⇒ NE-T on all traces. (Should be immediate from the definition of EA; if it isn't, the definitions are wrong.)

**Proof architecture:** NE-S is established by rely–guarantee: each component carries a contract (authority it may exercise; authority it may confer on others), contracts are shown stable under the steps of other contract-satisfying components, and composition preserves NE-S. NE-T is the exported guarantee.

## 5. Attenuation and revocation as refinements of the same invariant

- **Attenuation** is a transition where B(C) strictly narrows (task phase change, scope handoff to a sub-agent). No new machinery: it is the monotonicity rule with strict inequality at designated steps.
- **Revocation** is the narrowing of a *delegated capability's* contribution to ∅ mid-trajectory. Mechanism: delegated capabilities are routed through an indirection point (Redell's caretaker / Miller's membrane) owned by the grantor; clearing it removes ⟦c⟧ from the grantee's DA. The property-level content: after the revocation step, EA(grantee) must also shed ⟦c⟧ — i.e., revocation is *effective* only if no un-routed alias exists. **Q3:** is alias-freedom a structural invariant of the calculus (references to delegated caps are only ever caretaker-routed — preferred) or a runtime check (defeated by caching)?

Under this framing the project is one theorem (compositional NE-S/NE-T) with two corollaries (attenuation soundness; revocation effectiveness), not two disconnected results.

## 6. Scoping decision

Escalation here means **effects outside the bound**, not information escaping through permitted effects. A sub-agent with narrowed B can still signal secrets to a colluder via timing or choice among allowed effects; ruling that out is a hyperproperty (noninterference-like) and inherits McCullough's non-composability obstacle. Paper one states this scope explicitly, cites McCullough as the reason, and leaves the information-flow variant as future work.

## 7. Known weaknesses / positioning to check before freezing

1. **Prior art check:** Maffeis–Mitchell–Taly 2010 (authority safety via reachability) and Sewell et al. 2011 ("seL4 Enforces Integrity" — machine-checked authority-bound theorem) are the two closest results. The note's claim to novelty must be stated *relative to them*: plausibly (a) compositional rely–guarantee formulation for open multi-agent systems, (b) monotone bounds / mid-trajectory revocation as first-class, (c) agent-trajectory instantiation. Verify none of (a)–(c) is already done.
2. **Q1–Q3 above** are unresolved; the note is frozen only when each has a committed answer with a one-sentence justification.
3. EA's attribution clause (Q2) risks circularity with the definition of "induced"; the Alloy model exists to detect this.
